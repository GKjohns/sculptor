Sculpting skill pass 2: figures, poses, faces and busts

November 9 and 10, 2026. Branch figure-pass. Built offline first; nine paid runs at the end ($5.36 in all, section below). Every picture on this page is the canon on its own, expanded from a preset pose, set into the 60 x 100 x 60 cm block and either cut straight to the form or roughed out by the real hand (point at 3 cm, then claw at 0.6 cm), rendered with the look's contact-sheet renderer. Previews come from npm run bench:poses and npm run bench:canon.

What this page shows. The previews are the canons a model is handed, posed the way the prompt asks. The paid runs at the bottom show what models did with them unprompted: every figure run used declare_figure and chose an action pose, but they finished early, and Opus never found David's throw.

The brief

Kyle, after the face pass: "Don't be afraid to get more artistic and intricate. Like the kinds of things Bernini would sculpt." Full figures, animals and scenes; a horse rearing or mid-stride, not a standing box; a figure with contrapposto, torsion, gesture, drapery and a narrative moment; a bull charging with its head low. Midway the stopped face pass was folded in: busts, character in faces, lids and irises, hair, texture contrast.

What was built

  • A canon of the body (shared/sculpt/figure.ts) and a tool for it, declare_figure. The model gives a pose; each subject is built from jointed solids and expanded into kernel primitives before the form is logged, so KERNEL_VERSION stays 2. A human figure: body (man, woman, youth, child) and build; ground point, standing height and facing; legs (stand, stride, lunge, kneel, sit, recline, crouch, ride); weight_on (contrapposto: that hip up and out, the shoulders tilted against it, the free knee bent); twist of the shoulders against the hips; lean; head turn and tilt; nine arm gestures, each reached from the shoulder by a two-bone reach with the elbow bent toward a pole; drapery (loincloth, collar, mantle, cloak, robe); a stump or rock; and the head canon's style tokens. A quadruped: horse, bull, lion, dog, cat, in stand, walk, trot, gallop, rear, charge, lie or sit, head turned and raised or lowered, tail, lead foreleg. A rearing animal stands on its hind legs and its tail; a rider sits on the first animal. Up to three subjects plus 24 of the model's own shapes in one form. The result gives each subject's extent and its landmarks (head, hands, feet; muzzle, back, tail) so the model can lay a sling, a branch or a rock on the pose.
  • Pose by default in the prompt (a COMPOSITION AND POSE section, both tool sets) and the reference brief: choose the moment; name the dominant diagonal, the silhouette, the torsion, the gesture and the contrast of surfaces; a frontal, symmetric, static pose is a failure unless the commission asks for it; compose within the block (the height for figures and groups, the footprint's 85 cm diagonal for a long animal, a rock to raise it); supports where marble needs them. The brief no longer forbids outstretched limbs or raised tails, only free strands.
  • Faces and busts, starting from the stopped parallel pass (below): style tokens on the head, a torso canon, canon cuts struck by detail, a hair canon; then open eyes, crisp features, hooked locks, a classical trunk and cloth that wraps the body.
  • A per-solid blend in the kernel: a lid, a lip, a nostril wing or a lock keeps a crisp join on a softly blended body. Old logs carry none and replay identically.
  • Speed for big forms: an exact bounding-sphere cull (inherited), per-cell candidate lists and a sample memo for strike clipping, all giving the same numbers to the last bit; every saved study log still replays to its recorded hash.
  • Bench subjects david, bull, rider, with their own twelve judge labels that include the same subject at rest ("human figure standing at rest", "horse standing", "cow standing"), so a timid pose is scored as a miss. The study's twelve labels for pawn, cat and bust are unchanged.

Fixes after pass 3 (offline)

  • Sever guard. rough_out, detail and saw now run on a copy of the stone first. If a piece they would drop holds more than about a kilo of a declared form (told by stepping a centimetre in from the piece's surface and asking the forms; waste never counts), the pass is refused, the stone is left as it was, and the result says how much of which form would fall, where, and what to do instead. On bf4's own declaration, before the stem fix, the top detail is refused: "about 129 kg of "bust" around (-0.8, 53, 0) would fall". tests/sever.test.ts covers a synthetic case, a saw across the piece, and the bf4 replay.
  • The socle's stem is now a short column at least 11 cm across (a third of the shoulders) from the plinth well up into the chest, with a flared foot and a collar, whatever the declared truncation. No finishing pass can reach through it.
  • Bust proportions from the head (bustProportions): shoulders at least 2.2 head widths, the chest at least 0.8 of the head's depth, the trunk at least as tall as the head, the shoulders between a third of a head under the chin and the chin. The torso the model declared is changed to fit and the result says what changed. Run 662's declaration (a 28 cm trunk under a 40 cm head) becomes a trunk 40 cm tall and 59 cm wide (662-after).
  • Hair as one mass. Locks are flat ribbons laid on the hair mass with the form's own blend, so the silhouette is one shape with shallow valleys between locks; deeper separations only at the nape, undercuts under the fringe. Curls are flatter and blended. Grooves cut at the front read as vents at 0.4 cm and were dropped.
  • Lost runs. The bench records lost: true in run.json when under 5 percent of the block remains, and does not count the judge's pick; the report shows "lost" (and works it out from the last turn's mass for runs saved before).

Finish guard. A figure or bust commission (one that used declare_figure, or a head or torso canon) has its finish refused before 40 percent of its turns, at most twice, while under 70 percent of its money: the refusal quotes the commission back and asks for a last look from three sides, close-ups on the head and hands, and a note of what still does not answer the commission. Pawn and cat commissions finish as before. Not yet tried on a paid run.

Read. The tool works: every figure run declared its subject with declare_figure on the first or second turn, read the landmarks, re-declared to fit the block, and carved it without a refusal. Every run chose an action pose without being told: the bulls charge, the horses rear, the riders lean in. The weak point is the wind-up: Opus twice mapped David's sling to to_head, which reads as thought or a dance, even after the prompt named the throw's wind-up; Fable 5.2 found Bernini's pose at once. The bulls go on rocks taller than the prompt now allows. All runs finished early (7 to 20 of 60 turns, $0.44 to $1.04), roughing out and detailing the canon and then stopping, so what comes out is close to the canon's preview: the canon is now most of the quality. Busts repeat themselves across runs. Judges: 7 of 9 correct; both misses are the Opus Davids.

Not done, and weak

  • Few paid runs: nine, one or two per arm. Enough to see the tool used and poses chosen; not enough to rank models.
  • Focus is not persisted: it would need the loop's state and the worker protocol to carry it. Not cheap, left as it was.
  • Figure anatomy is a mannequin's: paddle hands, wedge feet, no knees or elbows to speak of, a chest of four masses. A figure under about 70 cm gets a three-solid head.
  • Animals: heads small and necks thin; the lion's mane hides its face; the horse's ears read as a horn from some sides; tails are tubes.
  • The block is fixed at 60 x 100 x 60. A long animal goes on the diagonal or on a rock; a per-commission block would serve the bull better.
  • Busts: faces are still siblings at a distance, the neck long from the side, breasts and shoulders too round.
