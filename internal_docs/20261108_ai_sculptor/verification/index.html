Sculptor: verification

An AI carves a block of virtual marble while a person watches. November 9, 2026.

This is the evidence page for the plan in implementation_plan.md. For each sprint it gives the plan's verification checklist, what was checked, the result, and the screenshots that back it. Every claim and number here comes from the plan's own record or from what is visible in a captured screenshot. Nothing was re-run to make this page, and where an item has no screenshot the page says so.

What has been built so far. The plan's goal is to type a commission, pick a stone and a model, and watch that model carve it start to finish in a cinematic real-time studio, ending with a finished piece and a replay you can scrub and export. Sprints 1 to 3 cover the first half of that: a tested carving kernel you can hit by hand in the browser, a headless study of whether models can carve at all, and a live studio where a typed commission is carved on screen with the storyboard's HUD. The stone is still the plain Sprint 1 render. The film look, the reveal, scrubbing and export are Sprints 4 and 5 and are not on this page yet.

Sprint 1: Stone you can hit

Goal. A block in the browser that you can carve by hand at 60 fps, backed by a tested kernel.

Status: complete. Local commit 6accbac, November 8, 2026.

Verification checklist

  1. Passednpm test passes.

    Result. 30 of 30 tests passed at the end of this sprint. They cover: a strike removes the expected volume within 5%; a protected shape is never cut; the mesh of a block with one corner chiselled is watertight across chunk seams; a sawn-off corner comes back as an offcut with the right volume; a thin bridge left standing is not detached.

    Caveat from the plan. The 5% figure holds for 4 cm strikes, which are within 3% of their true volume. 2 cm strikes (5 voxels across) can be 8% off.

    Evidence. No screenshot; verified by npm test per the plan.

  2. PassedIn the browser, 200 rapid strikes hold 60 fps on Kyle's Mac.

    Result. burst(200) on WebGPU at 120 Hz gave a mean frame time of 8.33 ms, p95 9.0 ms, max 9.4 ms, with no frame over 16 ms. burst(1000) gave the same.

    Caveat from the plan. These are vsync-quantised frame intervals. They show that no frames were dropped. They do not show how much main-thread headroom is left.

    Evidence. No screenshot; verified by __sculptor.burst(200) and burst(1000) in the page on WebGPU per the plan.

  3. PassedScreenshot: a block with a sawn corner and visibly different rough and smooth patches.

    Result. Captured on both backends. The plan records that the grid hash was the same on WebGPU and WebGL 2; it does not record the hash value.

    Evidence. The two demo-stone figures below. The hash comparison itself has no screenshot.

Kernel timings recorded in the plan (default grid, Node 22): one strike 0.2 ms, one chunk remesh 0.5 ms, island check 3 to 9 ms, full initial mesh 61 ms, a saw through half the block 80 to 160 ms in the worker. No screenshot.

Deviations and known rough edges

  • form log entry added. Protected cuts depend on declared forms, which were not in the strike log, so a replay could not reproduce them. The log gained a form entry that must come before any strike that names it.
  • Edge-contact cleanup. After every edit, stone that touches other stone only along a voxel edge has its shallower sample turned to air. Without it the mesher produced edges shared by four triangles.
  • Small strikes are less exact than 5%. See the caveat on checklist item 1.
  • The base is closed. Detached pieces under 0.5 cm³ are counted as removed but produce no offcut. The kernel uses its own polynomial sin and cos so logs replay across JS engines.
  • three is 0.187.0 and the post class is RenderPipeline. The palette is UFieldGroup because Nuxt UI 4 has no UButtonGroup. Finish is widened to Float32 on the main thread because WebGPU has no one-byte vertex format.
  • Offcuts tip out and fall under plain gravity, then are removed. Real physics is Sprint 4.
  • Remeshing is not time-sliced: a saw remeshes about 30 chunks in one flush. The plan says this is fine today and should be revisited in Sprint 4 if a slab fall drops a frame.
  • Finish-driven sheen only shows near the mirror angle because there is no environment light yet. Rough versus smooth currently reads from geometry and shadow, and the face away from the key light is very dark. Both are Sprint 4 lighting work.
  • shoot.mjs gained --eval, hides [data-hud] for its blank-canvas check, and fails on page errors or the wrong backend. The bench:sculpt script arrived in Sprint 2 with the bench.

What the study says.

  • Tool set: formed. Stroke-only carving never got past roughing on the cat or the bust and cost two to five times as much.
  • Reference: on. It fixed the cat's pose and made the bust fuller for $0.25 to $0.35 more per run. Sculptors also copy the sheet's mistakes, such as a tall pedestal.
  • Model. GPT-6 Sol made the best bust and the best-made cat at the lowest cost by using nearly all 60 turns, and is the slowest to watch (14 to 21 minutes). Opus 5.5 is the fastest (3 to 5 minutes) and good at masses, but wrecked faces and ears with fine chiselling. Fable 5.1 cost about $2 a run without doing better. Gemini 3.1 Pro made the most recognisable cat and the worst bust. Native tool calling worked on all four; the JSON-lines fallback was never needed.
  • Automatic gate criteria. They pass for Opus, Fable and Sol with formed tools and a reference: the judge was correct on both cat and bust, and every run cost under $2.10. The best bust (Sol) reads as a head and shoulders. It is a crude mannequin, not a portrait.

Gate decision (Kyle, November 9, 2026). Full (formed) tool set, reference on by default, Opus 5.5 as the default model, with Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Sol and Gemini 3.1 Pro in the picker. GPT-6 Sol made the best pieces in the study; Opus was chosen for its 3 to 5 minute pace. Its two weaknesses, stopping early and wrecking faces with fine chiselling, were given one bounded prompt-tuning pass at the start of Sprint 3 (result under Sprint 3). The original pass criteria were: for at least one configuration the judge picks correctly for both the cat and the bust, the bust reads as a head and shoulders to a person, and that run cost under $8.

The full report, with every run's contact sheets, costs and pass record: ../study/report.html.

Deviations and known rough edges

  • Code complete at 120 tests. Runs land in .data/study/<run-id>/ (gitignored).
  • Gateway key. sculptor-100 has a $100 hard cap and a 30-day expiry. The bench also keeps its own study-wide ledger and refuses to start a run that could pass $100.
  • Kernel version 2. Added the claw, a per-strike margin on protected cuts, and silhouette forms logged as a form entry so replays stay exact.
  • A full rough-out of the default block is about 15 saw cuts plus 1,400 chisel strikes, computed in about 2 s, leaving stone a mean 0.5 to 0.7 cm proud of the margin. No protected voxel was removed in any test.
  • rough_out refuses a target that floats or has a part not joined to the rest.
  • The rasp was rewritten. The first version could not smooth point-chisel pits. Five passes now take a real rough-out from 0.23 cm to 0.05 cm RMS roughness.
  • Reference sheets scale each view to the same height in block cm. Back and right outlines are the mirrored front and left ones.
  • Loop additions beyond the plan: a run ends as stalled after three turns with no tool call; budget warnings at 75% and 90% and a final-turn notice; invalid arguments come back to the model as an error result; the 90 s deadline and single retry live in the loop.
  • Cost accounting charges the larger of the price table and the gateway's own figure. --max-usd includes the reference and judge calls.
  • Problems that showed up across runs: fine chisel work destroys faces more often than it makes them; every run ended by the model's own choice, often very early (Opus stopped a cat at 9 of 60 turns); necks go missing and shoulders come out as bulbs; vertical comb ridges on nearly every surface; pieces left at half the block's height; the sculptor's finish statements describe features the stone does not have.
  • The automated judge is lenient. It gave 0.93 to a bust with a cratered face. Its pick is a floor, not a verdict on quality.
  • Stale line in the plan. The Sprint 2 notes still say native tool calling on GPT-6 Sol and Gemini 3.1 Pro was "not yet exercised with real calls". The study results, written after Round C, say it worked on all four models. The JSON-lines fallback was never needed, so that path has still not been exercised with real calls.

Deviations and known rough edges

  • Opus prompt tuning (one bounded pass, $3.68). Features are declared as small shapes in the form and roughed to at a fine margin; chisel strokes are for hollows only; a pacing rule and finish checklist were added. Result on the bust: full height, a real neck, square shoulders, and a face with brow, nose, eyes, mouth and intact ears (34 turns, $1.29, judge 0.96). The plan calls it still a caricature. Early stopping is improved, not fixed: the cat stopped at 24 turns and 59 cm. There is no screenshot of the tuned bust on this page.
  • Tools added: drill, chisel with roundel and a required protect argument, polish, slice, sketch, and, beyond the plan, closeup. All 16 study logs still replay to their recorded hashes and the kernel version stays 2. No screenshot.
  • Hanging stone. Kyle noticed pieces floating in mid-air because the island check only ran at the end of a pass. New passes log a check after every saw cut and every 2 s of carving; the longest hang went from 580 to 911 strikes down to 4 to 5. For older logs the browser meshes a display copy in which a hanging piece has already fallen. No screenshot.
  • Two kernel workers, one per stone, and a third worker for /api/brain. In one worker the hand froze playback for up to 2.7 s per rough-out, and posting the growing context from the page cost a 67 to 358 ms frame per turn.
  • Pacing rule: playback rate is backlog divided by drain time, clamped between a floor and 8× the chosen speed and eased over 0.5 s. The loop is held at its next tool call while paused or more than 60 s behind.
  • Sessions are files under .data/sessions/<id>/ with an append-only strikes.ndjson. Request bodies to /api/brain are capped at 24 MB; a Vercel deploy will need WebP looks to fit its 4.5 MB function limit (Sprint 5).
  • HUD departures from the storyboard: no "Sketch" stage, speed stops 1/4/8/20/60 on a slider, the overlay in the eye panel is a badge, and the scrubber and export wait for Sprint 5.
  • About 27 s of dead air before the first cut. The opening look was lengthened afterwards and this was not re-measured.
  • A turn in flight at reload is paid for but lost.
  • Charcoal. The plan says sketches are wiped at the next cutting pass rather than faded where carved. In the live roughing-out and staged-look frames above, charcoal lines are still on screen after cutting has started, some hanging in the air. Either those frames predate the wipe or the wipe does not cover this case; it has not been re-checked.
  • Restored sessions show past looks without images.
  • Each of a new log's frequent checks costs a full island check on the shown stone.
  • The plan says the model picker shows the short key ("opus"). The commission card in the frames above shows "Opus 5.5" when closed; the open list is not captured. The Nuxt DevTools pill overlaps the transport in dev.
  • Visible in the frames above and not in the plan: the tool badge is hard to read where it sits over the stone (wide live frames), the commission card covers the lower part of the block at 1400 px wide, and the finished plaque's statement is cut off behind the toolbar at 400 px wide.
